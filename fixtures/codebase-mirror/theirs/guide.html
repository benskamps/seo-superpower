How to send your first invoice

Ten minutes, start to finish. This walkthrough assumes you have a client and some tracked hours; if you have neither, start with the sample project and come back when a real one exists.

What do you need before you start?

Your tax registration number, the client billing address, a payment processor account, and your standard payment terms in days. Everything else has a sane default you can change later without reissuing anything.

How do you import clients from a spreadsheet?

Export your existing list to CSV with one column per field, then map columns on import. Billfold matches on email first and company name second, so a client with two contacts merges into a single record instead of duplicating.

Which tax fields are required?

Registration number, tax rate, and the reverse-charge flag for cross-border work in the EU and UK. Miss the reverse-charge flag and the invoice is legally wrong even though the arithmetic is right — this is the field people forget.

How should you set payment terms?

Fourteen days beats thirty for freelancers: the shorter clock gives you two reminder cycles inside the month you did the work. See the late payment guide for the cadence.

What happens after you hit send?

The client gets a link, not an attachment, so you can see when it was opened. Reminders fire on day three, day ten, and day fifteen unless payment lands.
